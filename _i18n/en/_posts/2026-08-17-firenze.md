Upon [my first visit to Florence](/blog/italia/) I didn’t get what all the fuss was really about. It appeared to be yet another European city with a cute historical center and vast art collections. Certainly above average, but not undoubtedly exceptional.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1290.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1207.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1183.jpg)

That was eons ago — I was in my twenties, with historical context slightly above that of a dung fly, and just enough cultural literacy to read a McDonald’s sign.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0600.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0686.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0503.jpg)

Yet I was expecting to get the infamous Stendhal syndrome right on the spot, which, of course, never happened — not on the first day, nor by the end of the trip.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0552.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0584.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0516.jpg)

Back then, even Genoa — an eclectic port town on the Ligurian coast — left a deeper mark. Probably because its features were more pronounced and apparent.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1418.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0603.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1409.jpg)

Understanding Florence turned out to be hard work that demanded either a degree in history or art, or a lot of unnecessarily sophisticated homework.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0181.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0193.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0205.jpg)

Revisiting Florence, I was approaching my forties, with most of Europe seen, a hundred more books read, and some major art galleries explored.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0561.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0563.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0577.jpg)

As an old fart, I gave up all hope of being sensitive enough for my long-overdue dose of the Stendhal syndrome, or at least some life-changing experience.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0525.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0537.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0538.jpg)

But I was finally able to appreciate the enormity of artistic power accumulated at a particular point in history, and the impact it had on our entire culture.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0174.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1096.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0179.jpg)

Florence basically nurtured the Renaissance itself, re-discovering the remnants of the bygone Roman civilization, and building the new epoch upon that foundation.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1197.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1199.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0766.jpg)

Merely listing names associated with the city sounds ridiculous: Dante, Boccaccio, Machiavelli, Botticelli, Galileo, and your favorite Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtle too.

- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0462.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0743.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0747.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0715.jpg)

It was only a century later that Rome — until then no more than a backwater town full of decaying ruins — took over, draining all creative resources of the peninsula.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0496.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1165.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0612.jpg)

Florence may have lost most of its influence after that, but had it kept the pace, we likely wouldn’t see many of its riches preserved in their origianal form.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0638.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0492.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0510.jpg)

With all that said, Florence can be too intense. After visiting five major galleries, plus a dozen basilicas, you’ll feel overwhelmed and mentally depleted.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0627.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0484.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0475.jpg)

If you’re not afraid of going down that perilous path, the [Firenze Card](https://www.firenzecard.it/) museum pass can at least save you some money.Unlike elsewhere, here it actually pays off.

- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1067.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1070.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1078.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0478.jpg)

If you ever need a break, you may wander off and explore the countryside with endless wineries and olive groves squeezed in between the many hills of Tuscany.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0217.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0277.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0335.jpg)

We were happy to spend our day off in Impruneta — a small town famous for its terracotta and olive oil. And it’s just a 30-minute bus ride from the city center.

- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0400.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0404.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0441.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0442.jpg)

After that trip I can easily place Florence among the top-10 cities I’ve ever visited, and certainly the most beautiful and saturated in this part of Europe.

![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0166.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0161.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1237.jpg)

Even the scorching heat of 38°C couldn’t spoil that impression, although I believe the beauty of such places reveals itself better in the colder seasons.

- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1029.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1031.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF0999.jpg)
- ![](/assets/images/2026/07/firenze/DSCF1133.jpg)

If you’re not into art and culture, that’s fine. Just get a gelato at [Barroccino](https://share.google/UccpxT3uhhMthzCs2), refill your bottle with free sparkling water [by the side entrance to Palazzo Vecchio](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Nnrcjb6PrwdxrY9WA), sit on the bank of the Arno, enjoy the view, and keep in mind you can revisit the city later in your life — when you’re ancient enough yourself to dig the boring dusty stuff.
